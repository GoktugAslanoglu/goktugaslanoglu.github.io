01 / Background

A little about me.

I’m interested in how AI systems use language and visual information to understand the world. My current research focuses on active vision in vision-language models (VLMs) and foveation—how models select and process visual information.

My recent work explores gaze-driven agents, optical memory, and visual grounding in multimodal retrieval. I also work on computational drug discovery through the CACHE7 challenge. After completing my current paper projects, I plan to study spiking neural networks (SNNs).

02 / Papers & publications

Selected research.

PosterSLM-Agents · 2026

Small Models, Exact Memories: Stress-Testing Optical Memory for Small Multimodal Agents

Göktuğ Aslanoğlu, Viswanadh Vadlamani, Şuayp Talha Kocabay

A zero-shot stress test of optical memory in small multimodal models. Selected visual evidence can be recovered, but dense rendered conversation histories remain unreliable for exact-state recall in the tested setting; text-based evidence performs better.

Posted on OpenReview 4 October 2026.

PosterHCV 2026

FOVA: Foveal Ordered Vision Agent

Göktuğ Aslanoğlu, Mehmet Kağan Albayrak

An active-perception framework that generates task-conditioned sequences of visual fixations. FOVA combines Gaussian foveation and Segment Anything Model (SAM) grounding with supervised fine-tuning and gaze preference optimization to explore human-inspired vision-language agents.

Posted on OpenReview 6 September 2026.

OpenReview PDF
Position paper · Non-archivalICML 2026 Workshop on EMM-QA

Visual Evidence Collapse as the Missing Signal in Adaptive Multimodal Retrieval: A Position

Göktuğ Aslanoğlu

Text-based confidence signals can miss when a vision-language model stops using visual evidence. This position paper proposes Visual Attention Trajectory (VAT), a training-free signal that tracks changes in attention to visual tokens, to help guide adaptive multimodal retrieval.

Accepted to the ICML 2026 Workshop on Efficient Multimodal Question Answering (EMM-QA).
